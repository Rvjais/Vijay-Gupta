Understanding Astrocytoma

Astrocytomas are tumors that develop from astrocytes – star-shaped glial cells that provide support and protection to the brain. These tumors can occur in the brain or spinal cord and range from benign (low-grade) to malignant (high-grade).

India has become a leading destination for astrocytoma treatment, offering world-class care with advanced surgical techniques. Grades 1 and 2 are slower-growing, while grade 3 and grade 4 (glioblastomas) are more aggressive. Dr. Vikas Gupta specializes in comprehensive diagnosis and personalized treatment planning.

Recognizing Astrocytoma Symptoms

warning

Headaches & Pain

Persistent headaches, often worse in the morning, due to increased intracranial pressure.

psychology

Seizures

Uncontrolled shaking or convulsions as the tumor irritates brain tissue.

visibility

Vision Problems

Blurred or double vision when the tumor affects areas controlling sight.

mood

Cognitive Changes

Memory problems, confusion, and personality changes may occur.

Early detection plays a vital role in effective treatment. Seek prompt medical attention for evaluation.

What Causes Astrocytoma?

01

Genetic Mutations

Mutations in the IDH1, IDH2, TP53, and ATRX genes are commonly associated with astrocytoma development.

02

Cellular Abnormalities

Abnormal growth and division of astrocytes lead to tumor formation. The exact trigger varies by tumor grade.

03

Environmental Factors

While most cases are not preventable, radiation exposure is a known risk factor for brain tumor development.

04

Age & Location

Astrocytomas are more common in adults between 45-65 years and typically develop in the cerebral hemispheres.

Frequently Asked Questions

Survival rates vary by grade. Low-grade astrocytomas (grades 1-2) have 5-year survival rates of 70-90%, while high-grade tumors (grades 3-4) require aggressive treatment with individualized prognosis.

Diagnosis involves MRI to visualize the tumor, CT scans for bone detail, and biopsy for histological grading. Molecular testing helps identify specific mutations for targeted therapy.

Low-grade astrocytomas can often be cured with complete surgical resection. Higher-grade tumors require ongoing management combining surgery, radiation, and chemotherapy.
